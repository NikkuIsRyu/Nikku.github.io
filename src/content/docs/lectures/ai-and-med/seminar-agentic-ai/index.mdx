## 🎙️ Presentation Guidelines & S5 Blueprint (Prof. Gertz)

Based on the seminar presentation guidelines (*"How to Give a Presentation"*, Prof. Dr. Michael Gertz / Prof. Dr. Jannik Strötgen):

### 1. Fundamental Rules
* **Pacing Rule of Thumb**: Plan for **1 to 2 minutes per slide**. For a strict 30-minute talk, your core presentation should have **18 to 22 content slides**.
* **"Tell 'em Three Times" Structure**:
  1. *Introduction*: Tell them what you are going to tell them (Motivation, Core Dilemma, High-level Roadmap).
  2. *Body*: Tell them (Methodology, Mathematical Formalization, Architecture, Empirical Results).
  3. *Conclusion*: Tell them what you just told them (Summary, Limitations, Critical Distance, Open Questions).
* **Maintain Scientific Distance**:
  * Never say *"in my paper"* or *"our approach"*. You did not author the paper. Use objective scientific attribution: *"Shinn et al. propose..."*, *"The authors evaluate..."*.
  * Dedicate explicit slides to **criticism, edge cases, and limitations**.
* **Formula Discipline**:
  * If you display an equation (e.g. $\theta = \{M_a, mem\}$ or policy $\pi_\theta$), explain **every single symbol and axis**. Never show uninterpreted mathematical boilerplate.
* **Strict GenAI Policy (Slide 9)**:
  * Prohibited: Generating slide text, slide layouts, or synthetic report text with GenAI.
  * Permitted: Literature discovery, terminology clarification, code syntax assistance, and grammar polish.

## 💬 Discussant Preparation: 4 Critical Questions for Paper S14 (AMIE)

When acting as designated discussant for **Paper S14** (*Towards Conversational Artificial Intelligence for Disease Management*, Nature 2026) on **Thursday, January 14, 2027**, use these prepared questions:

1. **Simulated OSCE vs. Real Clinical Reality**:
   > *"The evaluation is conducted on text-based Objective Structured Clinical Examinations (OSCEs) with simulated patient actors who communicate via clean text prompts. In real primary care, patients exhibit messy speech, omit vital symptoms, and present non-verbal signs (e.g., pallor, distress, tremor). How much of AMIE's apparent superiority over PCPs is an artifact of guideline-conforming text vignettes?"*
2. **Formulary Bias & Global Generalizability**:
   > *"AMIE's in-context retrieval relies heavily on the UK NICE guidelines and the British National Formulary (BNF). How well does this approach generalize to emerging economies or regional hospitals facing severe shortages of first-line medications, where clinicians must improvise off-label regimens?"*
3. **The Risk of Over-Adherence ("Guideline Anchoring")**:
   > *"AMIE scored significantly higher than human physicians on strict adherence to clinical guidelines. However, experienced physicians frequently and deliberately deviate from standard guidelines to accommodate idiosyncratic patient comorbidities or social constraints. Could AMIE's rigid adherence be a clinical liability in atypical presentations?"*
4. **Longitudinal Responsibility in Watchful Waiting**:
   > *"In multi-visit management, one of the most critical decisions is 'watchful waiting' versus active intervention. If AMIE recommends watchful waiting and a subtle disease exacerbation is missed between visits, how can clinical liability and verifiable audit trails be assigned in autonomous agentic care?"*
